The regions with the most mining and energy jobs have the most to lose from the transition

In Mangystau, Ulytau, Aktobe and Karaganda oblasts, more than one in ten workers is in mining or energy supply (2025 average), against 4.8% nationally. Study cities stand in for the oblasts they sit in.

Read with care. (1) BNS publishes this by oblast, not by city, so each study city stands in for its oblast; several cities share one (for example Karaganda, Temirtau and Balkhash all sit in Karaganda oblast). (2) “Mining and quarrying” includes metal ores and building materials, not only coal, oil and gas, so it overstates fossil-fuel dependence in some oblasts (Ulytau, for instance). “Energy supply” covers electricity, gas, steam and hot water. (3) The data come from a labour force survey, so small oblasts swing from quarter to quarter: that is why the 2025 annual figure is the safer view, and the latest quarter (Q3 2026) is offered to show how unstable it can be. (4) Abay, Jetisu and Ulytau were created in 2022, so comparisons with earlier years need care. Boundaries: 2024 administrative boundaries from the geokz R package (A. Rodionov).
