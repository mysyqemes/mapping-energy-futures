Decarbonizing Kazakhstan means replacing the backbone of its power grid

Coal supplied about 58% of Kazakhstan’s electricity in 2025 (Ember); other sources put it at 65–75%, depending on year and scope.

Chart: electricity generation by source, 2015–2025, TWh. Source: Ember (yearly generation data for Kazakhstan). Hover over the chart to read values.

Target line. The dashed line marks 15% of 2025 generation, the national goal for renewables in 2030. That goal excludes large hydropower (IEA, 2022; World Bank CCDR, 2022; reported also by Tengrinews, 2023). An interim goal of 6% by 2025 appears in the IEA review. The next chart compares how Ember and BNS measure progress against it. Note: the 2024 Carbon Neutrality Strategy does not restate this target; its “15%” is a greenhouse-gas reduction goal.

How close is Kazakhstan to the renewables goal? Two measurements

The same question, different answers: coal’s share of electricity

Three institutions report a coal share, and the numbers differ because the years, the grid covered and the definitions differ. The gap is part of the story, so it is shown rather than averaged away.

Is Kazakhstan a net importer of electricity?

Ember’s download does not include a trade series, so net imports are derived two ways: (a) BNS primary energy balance, electricity row (ktoe converted at 11.63 GWh per ktoe); (b) Ember demand minus Ember generation. They match to within 0.02 TWh for 2014–2024. The 2025 gap (0.45 TWh) is large and probably reflects a preliminary BNS balance, which should be checked. Positive means net imports. Kazakhstan flipped from net exporter to net importer in 2022.
